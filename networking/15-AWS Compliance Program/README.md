# Understanding Compliance

Compliance refers to following laws, regulations, policies, and industry standards that govern how organizations manage and protect information.

Organizations must demonstrate that they are implementing appropriate controls to:

- Protect sensitive data
- Ensure privacy
- Maintain operational security
- Reduce risk
- Meet legal obligations

---

# Why Compliance Matters

Compliance is important because it helps organizations:

- Protect customer data
- Reduce cybersecurity risks
- Avoid legal penalties
- Maintain customer trust
- Meet contractual obligations
- Support business continuity

Failure to comply with regulations can result in:

- Financial penalties
- Legal consequences
- Reputation damage
- Loss of customer confidence

---

# Laws, Standards, and Regulations

Although often grouped together, laws, standards, and regulations have different purposes.

---

# Laws

Laws are legal requirements established by governments.

Organizations must comply with laws that apply to their industry or region.

## Examples

- Data privacy laws
- Consumer protection laws
- Cybersecurity legislation

### Characteristics

- Legally enforceable
- Mandatory
- Violations may result in fines or legal action

---

# Regulations

Regulations are detailed rules created by regulatory authorities to enforce laws.

## Examples

- Financial regulations
- Healthcare security requirements
- Government security mandates

### Characteristics

- Industry-specific
- Mandatory compliance
- Often audited by regulators

---

# Standards

Standards are established best practices and frameworks developed by industry organizations.

## Examples

- ISO standards
- PCI DSS
- NIST frameworks

### Characteristics

- Sometimes voluntary
- Widely adopted
- Help improve consistency and security

---

# Common Regulatory Compliance Standards

Organizations using AWS may need to comply with various standards and frameworks.

---

# PCI DSS

## Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard

PCI DSS applies to organizations that process, store, or transmit payment card information.

### Focus Areas

- Cardholder data protection
- Access control
- Network security
- Monitoring and logging

### Industries

- Retail
- E-commerce
- Financial services

---

# HIPAA

## Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act

HIPAA protects sensitive healthcare information in the United States.

### Focus Areas

- Patient data privacy
- Medical record security
- Access controls
- Audit logging

### Industries

- Healthcare
- Insurance
- Medical services

---

# ISO 27001

ISO 27001 is an international standard for information security management systems (ISMS).

### Focus Areas

- Risk management
- Security controls
- Continuous improvement
- Information protection

### Benefits

- Globally recognized
- Demonstrates strong security practices

---

# SOC Reports

## System and Organization Controls (SOC)

SOC reports evaluate organizational security controls.

### Common Types

| SOC Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| SOC 1 | Financial reporting controls |
| SOC 2 | Security, availability, confidentiality |
| SOC 3 | Public-facing summary report |

---

# GDPR

## General Data Protection Regulation

GDPR is a European Union regulation focused on data privacy and protection.

### Focus Areas

- Personal data privacy
- Consent management
- Data breach reporting
- Data subject rights

### Applies To

Organizations processing data of EU residents.

---

# NIST Framework

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) provides cybersecurity frameworks and guidelines.

### Core Functions

- Identify
- Protect
- Detect
- Respond
- Recover

---

# FedRAMP

## Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program

FedRAMP standardizes security assessment and authorization for U.S. federal cloud services.

### Focus Areas

- Government cloud security
- Continuous monitoring
- Risk assessment

---

# AWS Compliance Program

AWS maintains a comprehensive compliance program designed to help customers meet regulatory and security requirements.

AWS continuously audits and certifies its infrastructure against global standards and frameworks.

---

# Key Components of the AWS Compliance Program

---

# 1. Security Certifications and Attestations

AWS undergoes independent third-party audits to validate compliance.

## Examples

- ISO certifications
- SOC reports
- PCI DSS compliance
- FedRAMP authorization

---

# 2. AWS Artifact

AWS Artifact provides on-demand access to AWS compliance reports and agreements.

## Available Documents

- SOC reports
- ISO certifications
- PCI compliance reports
- Security documentation

## Benefits

- Simplifies audit preparation
- Provides downloadable compliance evidence

---

# 3. Shared Responsibility Model

AWS compliance follows the shared responsibility model.

---

## AWS Responsibility

AWS is responsible for security **of the cloud**, including:

- Physical infrastructure
- Hardware
- Networking
- Data center security

---

## Customer Responsibility

Customers are responsible for security **in the cloud**, including:

- Data protection
- Identity and access management
- Operating system configuration
- Application security

# Monitoring and Continuous Compliance

Compliance is an ongoing process.

Organizations should continuously:

- Monitor resource configurations
- Audit user activity
- Review permissions
- Track security events
- Validate policy compliance

# Best Practices for Compliance on AWS

- Enable logging and monitoring
- Use encryption for sensitive data
- Apply least privilege access
- Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA)
- Perform regular security audits
- Monitor compliance continuously
- Use AWS Config rules
- Review Trusted Advisor recommendations
